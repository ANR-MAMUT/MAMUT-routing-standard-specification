\section{Store, compare and display a result}\label{sec:results}
A submission supplies the identified instance and its customer sequences. To store a Best-Known Solution (BKS), the checker validates the routes, recomputes the declared objective and writes the fields below. Store the routes in lexicographic order of their customer sequences. Keep the visit order within each route, including on symmetric matrices. If routes are reordered for storage, reorder their witnesses with them.

\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}lX@{}}
\toprule
JSON field & Contents \\
\midrule
\id{routes}, \id{cost} & Customer sequences with implicit depot endpoints, and the binary64 projection of the exact solution cost. \\
\multicolumn{2}{@{}l}{Inside \id{metadata.standard}:}\\
\id{specification}, \id{benchmark_revision} & Specification revision and immutable benchmark revision. \\
\id{manifest_sha256}, \id{routes_sha256} & Digests of the manifest file and the canonical route stream. \\
\id{profile}, \id{objective}, \id{num_routes} & Evaluation profile, declared objective and number of routes. \\
\id{authoritative_cost} & Exact total, encoded as a reduced fraction. \\
\id{display_overflow} & True exactly when the binary64 projection overflowed. \\
\id{evaluation} & Actual backend, checker identity, immutable source revision or build digest, runtime identity and verification status. Record the backend actually selected even when the caller used the default. \\
\id{route_evaluations} (TD) & One object per stored route, in the same order. Both \id{duration} and \id{departure_time} are required exact fractions. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}

Represent every exact fraction as an object with \id{numerator} and \id{denominator} strings containing base-10 integers in lowest terms. The denominator is positive. Omit leading plus signs and redundant leading zeros. Zero is the pair of strings \id{"0"} and \id{"1"}. Reject a noncanonical encoding, a missing or misaligned TD witness, or any asserted exact value inconsistent with recomputation, including a feasible departure later than the earliest minimizer. A resource failure does not establish infeasibility.

The route digest is SHA-256 of an ASCII stream beginning with \id{MAMUT-ROUTES-v1} and LF, then the route count and LF. For each stored route append its customer count, a colon, the comma-separated customer indices and LF. Write integer tokens in base 10 without leading zeros. For instance, the line for $\langle 2,1\rangle$ is \id{2:2,1} followed by LF.

\paragraph{Reading the companion examples.}
The static fixture has three arc costs read from \id{0.1}, \id{0.2} and \id{0.3}. Their exact sum is $21617278211378381/36028797018963968$ and the displayed \id{cost} is \id{0.6}. The TD fixture has two routes. Their earliest departures are $10/3$ and $5$, and their minimum durations are $14/3$ and $6$. With a charge of $5/4$ per vehicle the exact solution cost is $79/6$. The first entry in its \id{route_evaluations} array reads
\begin{verbatim}
{"duration":{"numerator":"14","denominator":"3"},
 "departure_time":{"numerator":"10","denominator":"3"}}
\end{verbatim}
The companion includes both complete JSON results with their instances, manifests and digests.

Compare candidates only for the same benchmark revision, interpreted inputs, objective and evaluation profile. Use the complete exact objective key, including the vehicle-count tier where applicable. Replace the incumbent on a strict improvement. On equality keep the stored incumbent. Distinct exact costs with the same displayed number still compare by their exact values.

Round the exact total once to the nearest binary64 with ties to even and serialize a finite value as a round-trip JSON number, using positive zero. On overflow write \id{cost:null} and set \id{display_overflow} to true, preserving the exact fraction. A website showing a fixed number of decimal places rounds directly from the exact fraction to that many places, with ties to even. Displayed departure times use the same binary64 rounding rule. For example, on the \href{https://github.com/0nyr/routing-numerical-contracts/blob/75905c8827f959621ea808894e28136d248d2d15/results/rational-panel.json}{archived Blauth-Nairobi 10-customer route}, the exact earliest departure is $46757961125227679073/790061513395$ ms. It reaches the second customer at its deadline. Replaying its rounded display value \id{59182684.30049512} ms misses that deadline by about $9.73\times10^{-10}$ ms. Use the exact witness for replay.
