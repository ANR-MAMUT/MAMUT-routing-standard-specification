\section{Attainment of the minimum route duration}\label{app:td-attainment}

Fix a customer order and its ATFs under Section~\ref{sec:td-core}, and let $D_{\mathbf r}$ denote the set of feasible depot departures. Its initial domain is the intersection of a closed departure horizon and any depot departure window. On this interval the initial time map is the identity. A FIFO arc with the lower value attained at each jump is nondecreasing and left-continuous, and the customer map $u\mapsto\max(u,e_j)+s_j$ has the same two properties. Composing two such maps preserves both properties on the feasible domain, since the inner map approaches its value from below or is constant.

Nonnegative travel and service preserve the lower horizon bound at each step, whereas a latest-service constraint, the upper bound on an arc departure or a depot return deadline restricts a prefix time to a closed upper bound. By monotonicity, the preimage of such a bound within the current domain is an initial interval, and left continuity includes its right endpoint whenever that interval is nonempty. By induction, $D_{\mathbf r}$ is therefore empty or a compact interval, possibly reduced to a singleton.

A nondecreasing left-continuous return map can jump only upward immediately to the right of a point, which makes it lower-semicontinuous. Subtracting the continuous departure time preserves this property, so the duration attains its minimum on every nonempty $D_{\mathbf r}$, and its set of minimizers is compact and has a smallest member.

All knots, service durations and window bounds are rational. Within each finite affine prefix piece, the preimage of a boundary is either a rational cut, the entire piece or the empty set. There are consequently finitely many rational cuts, with an affine duration on each intervening open interval. A nonconstant affine piece has no interior minimum, while on a constant minimizing piece lower semicontinuity and closedness of the feasible domain include its left endpoint among the minimizers. The earliest minimizer is therefore a rational cut or domain endpoint, and evaluating the route there yields a rational duration.
